How the top 7 are chosen

Every week around 2,300 articles come in from our sources. The digest shows 7 per category, picked for someone working in fashion, jewellery or ecommerce who wants the news that matters, well reported, and never an ad. The numbers below are from the week 45 build (2–8 November 2026).

Who does the work: Rule fixed code, no AI Embeddings text similarity Luna fast, cheap model Sol strong model
  1. In

    The week's articles

    2,268 articles

    Everything ingested from the news feeds, Monday to Sunday, with a short summary each.

  2. 1

    Hard filters

    Rule
    2,268→2,197

    Removes obvious advertising before any AI reads it: sponsored URL paths, press-release wire services, and titles labelled "Sponsored", "Advertorial", "Partner content" or "Promoted". Articles with no usable text go too.

    Cut 71: 7 sponsored pages, 64 with no text.
  3. 2

    Group into stories

    Embeddings
    2,197 articles→2,052 stories

    Articles about the same event are grouped so a story counts once. When several outlets cover the same story, that coverage counts towards its significance.

    82 stories were covered by two or more outlets.
  4. 3

    Triage every article

    Luna
    2,197→1,600 in a category

    A fast model reads the headline and summary of every article and records four things: the category, the type (news, analysis, opinion, press release, sponsored…), how significant it is for an industry reader on a 1–5 scale, and how likely it is to be an ad.

    Cut 597 as off-topic. Also flagged: 134 press releases or sponsored posts and 102 podcasts or event promos, which cannot enter the shortlist.
  5. 4

    Shortlist

    Rule
    1,600→100 (25 per category)

    Ranks by significance, article type and coverage. Keeps one article per story and spreads the sources: at most 4 from one publication and 5 from one website.

    Cut 1,500. This is the biggest cut, and it is made on the triage scores.
  6. 5

    Read the full article

    Rule
    100→84 full text + 16 summary only

    Downloads the article itself so the next step judges the reporting, not the teaser. Detects paywalls, and checks the body for disclosures such as "sponsored by" or "in partnership with".

    The 16 were paywalled or blocked, so they are judged on their summary.
  7. 6

    Quality judge

    Sol
    100 scored

    A strong model scores each article from 1 to 5 on five things, flags hidden ads, and writes one line on why the story matters. Length earns no points.

    35%20%20%15%10%
    SignificanceSubstanceInsightIndep.Writing
    • Substance: new facts, figures, named sources, original reporting.
    • Insight: explains why it happened and what comes next.
    • Independence: 5 is independent journalism, 1 is an advertorial or PR copy.
  8. 7

    Editor's pick

    SolRule
    100→28 (7 per category)

    The strong model chooses and orders seven stories per category, the way an editor would. Fixed rules then check its picks and swap out any that break them:

    • Ranks 1–5 must clear the lead barsignificance ≥ 4 · quality ≥ 3.4 · independence ≥ 3 · no hidden ad
    • One article per event, even when outlets frame it differently
    • At most 3 from one publication and 2 from one website
    • At most 2 stories about the same company in the top 5
    • At most 1 paywalled story in the top 3

Blind test, old vs new

7 of 8

A model from a different company compared old and new top-7 lists without knowing which was which, twice per category with the order swapped. The new funnel won all 4 categories in week 45 and 3 in week 43. The fourth (Jewellery) was a tie.

Safety nets

  • If an AI step fails, a simpler fallback fills in and that step is marked in the build report.
  • If the whole funnel fails, the build uses the previous selection method, so a digest always ships.
  • Article text is treated as data. Instructions hidden inside an article are ignored.
