Hands-On: Chronoswiss PULSE GMT Silver Guilloché Offers A Fresh Take On The Travel Watch 

September 8, 2026
Add Comment
by Ripley Sellers

One of the most noteworthy releases from Chronoswiss in recent years has been the introduction of its PULSE collection, which originally debuted in 2025 as the Swiss brand’s first integrated bracelet model. Rather than adhering to the familiar 1970s Genta-inspired design codes that frequently serve as the template for this popular genre of timepieces, the PULSE lineup has its own distinct style that represents a thoroughly modern expression of the brand’s foundational aesthetic. Earlier this year in 2026, Chronoswiss expanded the PULSE collection with models that offer a unique approach to dual-time functionality, and featured here is the Chronoswiss PULSE GMT Silver Guilloché, which is a 200-piece limited edition crafted from grade 5 titanium.

The Chronoswiss PULSE GMT Silver Guilloché (ref. CH-4223TM-GR) is from the original duo of PULSE GMT models that were unveiled earlier this year at Watches & Wonders 2026. At the time of writing, Chronoswiss currently offers three different versions of the PULSE GMT, and all three of them are limited editions. The original duo consists of the titanium “Silver Guilloché ” model featured here (200 pieces) and its “Sky Gold” sibling that is crafted from solid 18k gold and fitted with a blue sky-pattern enamel dial (50 pieces). However, Chronoswiss has since released a third iteration of the Pulse GMT that pairs an 18k gold case with a silver frosted guilloché dial (also a limited edition of 50 pieces), and rather than being fitted with an integrated bracelet like its two siblings, the latest solid-gold model is paired with a black rubber strap.

The design of the Chronoswiss PULSE GMT is an extension of the brand’s original PULSE One watches, and their cases and bracelets largely following in the footsteps of what can be found among their single-timezone siblings. Certain details that are emblematic of Chronoswiss have been reimagined in a decidedly contemporary (almost futuristic) manner, such as flowing guards surrounding the brand’s signature onion-shaped crown, and bold notches replacing the delicate coin-edge fluting that typically appears along the upper and lower edges of Chronoswiss cases. Despite being significantly more modern than the rest of the brand’s catalog, the PULSE collection still feels like a natural expansion of Chronoswiss’ foundational design language, and the PULSE GMT takes the concept one step further with the ability to track a second timezone.

The Chronoswiss PULSE GMT Silver Guilloché uses grade 5 titanium for both its case and bracelet, and the vast majority of its components are given either a brushed or sandblasted finish, with only a few small high-polished details appearing throughout the watch. A curved sapphire crystal protects the dial, while a flat sapphire crystal serves as the display window in the screw-on caseback, and unlike its solid-gold siblings that have 50-meter depth ratings, the Silver Guilloché version of the PULSE GMT offers a solid 100 meters of water resistance. With that in mind, dimensions remain the same at 41mm in diameter by 13mm thick and 46.27mm in the lug-to-lug direction, although the “lugs” of the PULSE GMT are actually the end-links of the bracelet, which mount directly into the side of its middle case to create a fully integrated structure.

Instead of having dials with a vertically-oriented regulator layout like the brand’s PULSE One models, the PULSE GMT draws its inspiration from the Chronoswiss Tora watches of the 1990s, and it tracks its two time zones with a pair of independently-adjustable hour hands located on either side of its domed multi-component dial, which are used in conjunction with its centrally-mounted minute and seconds hands. The hour hand at 3 o’clock opts for a conventional 12-hour format to display the local time, while the hand at 9 o’clock is dedicated to home time with a 24-hour format to differentiate between AM and PM hours, and the pair of hour hands sit at the top of highly convex registers that create an immense sense of visual depth as they extend into the dial’s curved surface.

To complement its gray titanium exterior, the Chronoswiss PULSE GMT Silver Guilloché is fitted with a silver dial, framing a central section adorned with guilloché inside an outer ring that has a dark sandblasted finish. The dial’s curved surface echoes the shape of the heavy domed registers that are set into its structure, and the convex shape further accentuates the appearance of its intricate guilloché motif. All of the printing on the dial is executed in black, although metallic blue accents punctuate the display in the form of blued hands, a pair of blued screws in the center of the dial, and small blue indexes set within the minute track. The overall appearance of the PULSE GMT strikes a nice balance of being harmonious yet distinct, and similar to other PULSE GMT models, each example of the Silver Guilloché version has its individual limited-edition number out of 200 examples printed at the bottom of its dial.

Visible through the display caseback of the Chronoswiss PULSE GMT Silver Guilloché is the brand’s manufacture Caliber C.6002 automatic movement, which operates at a rate of 28,800vph (4 Hz) with a 55-hour power reserve. Decorated with Geneva stripes radiating outward from its center, the 29-jewel Caliber C.6002 winds itself with skeletonized rotor crafted from tungsten, and its components are given a ruthenium plating that nicely matches the model’s predominantly gray color profile. Along with featuring the standard conveniences of hacking-seconds and manual-winding through the crown, the Chronoswiss Caliber C.6002 also offers a secondary timezone display, but it functions slightly differently than other GMT-equipped movements.

Since the Chronoswiss PULSE GMT Silver Guilloché doesn’t include a date display, operating the watch is surprisingly straightforward. Pulling the crown out to the first setting position offers independent adjustment of the secondary 24-hour hand, which can either be set forward or backward by simply rotating the crown. Meanwhile, the second setting position allows you to adjust the time (similar to most watches), and the pair of hour hands (plus the minute hand) will remain synchronized as you rotate them around the dial. The one slight quirk is that the 24-hour hand moves freely during independent adjustment (rather than jumping in one-hour increments), which means that achieving perfect alignment can be slightly challenging unless you happen to be setting the secondary timezone right at the start of an hour.

Crafted from grade 5 titanium and tapering from 23mm at the case down to 18mm on the underside of the wrist, the integrated bracelet for the Chronoswiss PULSE GMT Silver Guilloché matches the watch’s distinctly modern lines, with a sculpted design and small high-polished accents set within the recessed center sections of its links. Rather than being a fluid object that drapes around the wrist, the PULSE GMT’s bracelet is somewhat rigid, and it forms a cuff-like structure in a manner that is similar to what can be observed among other popular integrated bracelet watches like the Audemars Piguet Royal Oak. The clasp itself is a fairly standard butterfly-style affair that doesn’t offer any type of micro-adjustment system, but Chronoswiss has taken advantage of its svelte design by machining the underside of the adjacent bracelet links so that the clasp’s components sit within their structure.

In recent years, widespread demand for both GMT watches and integrated bracelet sports models has resulted in countless different brands releasing their own versions of these popular styles of timepieces. Some collectors are now starting to experience a certain degree of fatigue when it comes to these two fan-favorite genres from horology. That said, the Chronoswiss PULSE GMT manages to stand out as a unique offering in both of these densely populated (and fiercely competitive) categories, as it approaches dual-time functionality in a manner that is tangibly different from other styles of GMT watches, while simultaneously representing a fresh visual departure from the typical Genta-derived designs that influence the majority of today’s integrated bracelet models.

Within the context of both dual-time watches and models with integrated bracelets, the Chronoswiss PULSE GMT manages to feel distinct, and that is no small feat given the number of options that now exist within these two categories. At the same time, the model also feels like a natural extension of Chronoswiss’ core identity that re-contextualizes the Swiss brand’s hallmark design elements through a definitively contemporary lens. Priced at $20,300 USD and produced as a limited edition of 200 examples, the Chronoswiss PULSE GMT Silver Guilloché (ref. CH-4223TM-GR) is the least expensive model among the current trio of PULSE GMT watches (by a significant margin), and it represents a fresh and elevated take on the travel watch concept. Fore more information, please visit the Chronoswiss website.
