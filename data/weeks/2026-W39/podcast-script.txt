Our first story this week is about a brewing rivalry at the intersection of ecommerce and artificial intelligence. Amazon has made headlines by blocking Meta’s new AI shopping agent, Muse, from browsing and purchasing products on its platform. This move signals the first major turf war of what industry insiders are calling “agentic shopping”—where AI agents act on behalf of consumers to find, compare, and even purchase products autonomously. Meta’s Muse is designed to streamline online shopping for users, potentially taking the tedious task of searching, comparing prices, and finalizing purchases off their hands. However, Amazon’s decision to restrict Muse’s access reflects a deeper concern about control—both over customer data and the actual shopping experience. By shutting out Meta’s agent, Amazon is drawing a firm line, suggesting it intends to keep its ecosystem closed to outside AI intermediaries. This could have far-reaching implications for the future of online shopping, as agentic commerce promises to revolutionize how consumers interact with retail platforms. At stake is not just convenience, but also the power dynamics between tech giants. Will consumers demand more freedom to use third-party AI agents, or will platforms keep doubling down on walled gardens? The answer could shape the next era of ecommerce. Amazon’s move is a clear message: the battle for who gets to mediate the online shopping journey has only just begun, and the rules of engagement are far from settled.

Speaking of divergent strategies in the world of ecommerce platforms, Shopify has taken a very different approach to Meta’s Muse. In contrast to Amazon’s defensive stance, Shopify has welcomed integration with Muse, opening its doors to agentic commerce. This decision highlights a significant split in philosophy among leading ecommerce players. While Amazon appears focused on maintaining strict control over its ecosystem, Shopify is betting on openness and collaboration. By allowing Muse to access its platform, Shopify is positioning itself as a more flexible, partner-friendly marketplace—potentially gaining favor with tech-savvy merchants and shoppers alike. This move could accelerate the adoption of AI-powered shopping assistants, since Shopify’s vast network of independent merchants stands to benefit from increased traffic and streamlined transactions. For Meta, Shopify’s cooperation is crucial, as it provides a major avenue for Muse to gain traction and prove its worth in the real world. The contrast between Amazon and Shopify’s responses underscores a broader conversation about the future of ecommerce—will it be dominated by a few closed ecosystems, or will interoperability and third-party innovation drive growth? For brands and retailers, the answer will shape their digital strategies and influence how they connect with customers. For consumers, it could determine whether AI agents like Muse become a routine part of the shopping experience, or remain limited by platform restrictions. As agentic commerce becomes a reality, the willingness of platforms to partner—or compete—will dictate the pace of innovation in online retail.

Now, let’s pivot from AI battles to a story that’s quite literally taking flight: Lowe’s has launched drone delivery for online orders, in partnership with Wing and DoorDash. This bold move brings the promise of near-instant delivery to reality, offering select products to both DIY enthusiasts and professional customers in as little as 20 minutes. The service, which is rolling out in select markets, allows customers to order hardware, tools, and home improvement essentials and have them delivered to their doorstep by drone—a futuristic leap forward in convenience. By teaming up with Wing, a leader in drone technology, and DoorDash, a household name in last-mile delivery, Lowe’s is positioning itself at the forefront of logistics innovation. The implications extend beyond just faster delivery times; drone delivery could help retailers reach customers in areas where road traffic or geography make traditional delivery slower or more costly. It also signals a broader shift in customer expectations—instant gratification is no longer a luxury but increasingly the norm. As drone technology matures and regulatory hurdles are addressed, we could see this model expand rapidly, especially for small, high-value items. For Lowe’s, it’s an opportunity to differentiate itself in a fiercely competitive sector, while for the broader industry, it’s a sign that logistics is becoming a key battleground for customer loyalty. Over time, drone delivery has the potential to reshape urban and suburban retail, making online orders as fast—or faster—than a trip to the store.

Let’s shift gears to the world of jewellery, where branding and storytelling are undergoing a transformation. The Natural Diamond Council, the industry body tasked with promoting natural diamonds, has announced a major rebrand: it will now be known as The Diamond Collective. This change is more than cosmetic—it reflects a strategic effort to reposition natural diamonds in a market increasingly crowded by lab-grown alternatives and shifting consumer values. The new name is part of a global marketing campaign aimed at uniting the trade and reigniting consumer desire for natural diamonds. The Diamond Collective hopes to foster a sense of community and shared purpose among miners, retailers, and consumers, emphasizing the heritage, rarity, and emotional significance of natural stones. This rebranding comes at a critical time for the diamond industry, as younger generations weigh ethical, environmental, and value considerations when making jewellery purchases. The Diamond Collective’s campaign is expected to highlight the journey of each stone and the positive social impact of responsible mining. By reframing the conversation around natural diamonds, the industry hopes to defend its market share and reinvigorate demand. For retailers, this rebrand offers new storytelling opportunities, while for consumers, it aims to restore the mystique and desirability historically associated with natural diamonds. The move underscores the importance of brand identity and narrative in luxury, especially as the competitive landscape evolves.

Staying in the realm of jewellery, Selfridges has just launched a year-long residency for the contemporary jewellery brand Poubel at its flagship Oxford Street store in London. This partnership marks a significant step for both Selfridges and Poubel, as it brings cutting-edge design and craftsmanship to one of the world’s most iconic luxury retail spaces. The residency will introduce Selfridges’ broad and diverse clientele to Poubel’s distinctive aesthetic, known for its bold forms and innovative use of materials. For Selfridges, hosting a year-long residency is more than just a retail arrangement—it’s a statement about its commitment to championing fresh talent and fostering creativity within the jewellery sector. The initiative is expected to draw new customers seeking unique, statement pieces, while also offering established collectors an opportunity to engage with emerging design trends. By spotlighting Poubel, Selfridges is reinforcing its reputation as a curator of avant-garde luxury and a destination for discovery. For the industry, this residency highlights the growing importance of experiential retail and the need for brick-and-mortar stores to differentiate themselves in an increasingly digital world. The partnership also illustrates how luxury retailers can use residencies and collaborations to keep their offerings dynamic and relevant, ensuring that the in-store experience remains compelling even as ecommerce continues to surge.

Turning our attention to online fashion, Zalando recently gathered its brand partners to discuss a pressing topic: what artificial intelligence can—and cannot—do for the fashion business. During its annual Partner Day, Zalando outlined how it is leveraging AI to cut operational costs, generate marketing content, and analyze customer data to personalize the shopping experience. However, Zalando was also candid about the current limitations of AI, particularly when it comes to the nuances of fashion curation, trend forecasting, and the deeply personal nature of style. While AI excels at logistical optimization and large-scale data analysis, it still struggles with the creative and emotional aspects that define the fashion industry. Zalando’s message to its partners was clear: AI is a powerful tool, but it is not a silver bullet. The company emphasized the importance of combining technological innovation with human expertise—merging data-driven insights with the intuition and creativity of designers and merchandisers. For brands, this means that AI can help streamline operations and unlock new efficiencies, but success in online fashion still depends on storytelling, brand identity, and understanding the evolving desires of consumers. Zalando’s openness about the strengths and weaknesses of AI reflects a pragmatic approach that could serve as a model for the industry. As AI technology continues to advance, the challenge will be to harness its capabilities without losing sight of the artistry and individuality that make fashion unique.

As we move from fashion to the broader luxury sector, let’s discuss an eye-opening analysis from Daniel Langer’s Luxury Unfiltered column: according to recent data, a staggering 80 percent of luxury sector growth has been driven by price increases, rather than volume expansion. This finding has significant implications for brands, investors, and consumers alike. It suggests that the recent boom in luxury has been less about attracting new customers or selling more products, and more about extracting greater value from existing clientele through higher prices. This strategy has served brands well in the short term, driving revenue growth and reinforcing exclusivity. However, it also raises questions about the long-term sustainability of the sector’s growth model. Relying heavily on price hikes could risk alienating aspirational consumers and eroding goodwill, particularly if economic conditions become less favorable. For investors, the data underscores the need to look beyond headline growth figures and examine the underlying drivers of performance. For consumers, it may prompt reflection on the value proposition of luxury goods amid escalating prices. The analysis also spotlights the delicate balance luxury brands must strike between maintaining exclusivity and ensuring accessibility. As competition intensifies and consumer expectations evolve, the industry may need to explore new sources of growth—such as experiential offerings, digital engagement, or sustainability initiatives—rather than relying solely on pricing power.

And now, let’s talk about the staggering scale of investment being poured into artificial intelligence infrastructure. According to Goldman Sachs, Big Tech companies—including Amazon, Alphabet, Microsoft, Oracle, and Meta—are projected to spend an astonishing $1.2 trillion on AI infrastructure by 2027. This figure dwarfs previous Wall Street estimates and represents a more than 50 percent increase over projected 2026 levels. The implications of this investment are profound. Such massive spending will accelerate the development and deployment of AI technologies across industries, from ecommerce and logistics to healthcare, finance, and beyond. For tech giants, building out robust AI infrastructure is not just about staying competitive—it’s about defining the future of digital business and securing a dominant position in the next era of computing. For the broader economy, this investment wave is likely to spur innovation, create new markets, and reshape the competitive landscape. However, it also raises important questions about concentration of power, data privacy, and the societal impacts of rapidly advancing AI capabilities. For retailers and brands, the message is clear: the pace of technological change is accelerating, and staying ahead will require both strategic vision and significant investment. As AI becomes ever more integrated into the fabric of commerce and daily life, the stakes—for businesses, consumers, and society at large—have never been higher.
